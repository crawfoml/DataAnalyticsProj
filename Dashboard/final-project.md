## Purpose

Choose a real-world dataset and conduct an end-to-end exploratory data analysis
that demonstrates the concepts and practices developed throughout the course.
Your analysis should be question-driven, iterative, and non-linear.

## Dataset requirements

- Attribute the source of the data.
- Prefer a dataset relevant to your work, research, or interests, but do not
  use sensitive information or anything that should not be shared.
- Choose non-trivial data. As a default target, prefer at least 5,000
  observations and 10 useful features; ask for approval before using a smaller
  dataset.
- Avoid datasets with so many features that a careful analysis is impractical.
- Do not use datasets already used in this course or its textbooks, datasets
  bundled with common Python data-science or machine-learning packages, or
  other widely used toy or educational datasets.
- Your analysis must be your own work, even when the data are publicly
  available.

## Analysis expectations

Apply the course EDA workflow:

1. Identify the questions you want to answer.
2. Load the data and perform initial reconnaissance.
3. Assess data quality.
4. Make and justify cleaning decisions.
5. Conduct statistical and visual EDA.
6. Transform or engineer features where useful.
7. Save and document the resulting analysis.

The bulk of the work should be in one or more notebooks. The submission should:

- demonstrate an overall understanding of the data;
- identify and address issues involving types, missing or invalid values,
  outliers, and other quality concerns;
- find informative patterns and relationships;
- engineer useful features where appropriate;
- generate and explore hypotheses without requiring formal statistical tests;
- combine qualitative and quantitative reasoning;
- use the notebook format effectively by integrating prose, code, and results;
- apply the principles and practices covered in the course.

Graduate students must also create an interactive Streamlit dashboard suited
to the questions and data being explored.

## Deliverables

- The URL of a dedicated GitHub repository containing the group's work.
- The data, analysis notebooks, dashboard source where required, a suitable
  README, and a coherent repository structure.
- For graduate students, a five- to ten-minute prerecorded presentation focused
  on the analysis and findings rather than a line-by-line walkthrough.
- Constructive feedback on other teams' presentations through the associated
  discussion activity.

## Evaluation

- Science—questions and findings: 20 points
- Engineering—process, validation, and verification: 20 points
- Skills—implementation: 38 points
- Professionalism—overall quality: 10 points
- Ambition—work that meaningfully exceeds expectations, especially in the
  dashboard: up to 5 bonus points

AI tools may be used in support of the work, but every team member must be able
to explain the implementation and justify the analytical decisions.
